# Analysis and interpretation

The main purpose of this session is to produce the first meaningful result and understand what it tells us.

This is be the main 'practical' session of these online sessions.
You work through your proposed workflows, with support available via the online meeting.

The session consists of three stages.

## Run the analysis
### 13:30–14:45 (75 min)
Students continue their chosen mini-project, following the workflow they developed in the morning. The tasks will differ by project.
Project type. Examples of tasks:
- RNA-seq	Check reads, map to a reference, quantify expression, investigate differential expression
- Genome assembly	Assemble reads, inspect contigs, assess assembly quality
- Open-ended project	Implement the first steps of an agreed, feasible workflow
Use the supplied datasets and existing Galaxy workflows wherever possible, rather than spending the session downloading huge datasets or installing software.

## Turn outputs into figures
### 15:00–15:45 (45 min)
Try to identify at least one result worth communicating.
Examples might include:
- An RNA-seq PCA plot or volcano plot.
- A table of candidate differentially expressed genes.
- A genome assembly summary table.
- A plot or table showing contig lengths or assembly completeness.
  
The important point is not the number of figures. It is whether you can explain what a figure shows and why it is relevant to your biological research question.

## Interpret the result
### 15:45–16:30 (45 minutes)
Try to answer three questions in writing:
1. What does my result show?
2. What does it not show?
3. What should I do next?

For example:
- If you are identifying differentially expressed genes, you should consider whether the result is statistically supported, whether the sample metadata justify the comparison, and whether the genes have plausible biological interpretations.
- If you are assembling a genome, you should distinguish contiguity from completeness and avoid assuming that a large N50 necessarily means a good assembly.

The **deliverable**: one preliminary figure and its interpretation

## Checkpoint
By the end of Thursday, you should have:
- At least one preliminary result.
- A figure or table.
- A short explanatory caption.
- A few sentences explaining the result and its limitations.
